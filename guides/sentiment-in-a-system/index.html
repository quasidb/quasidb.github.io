QDB Guide · Using QDB in a trade · about 10 minutes

Sentiment: the final layer of any trading system

The question we're asked most is "how do I use your tool to put a trade on?" The honest answer: QDB isn't one tool, and it isn't a signal service. Retail sentiment is a trigger to execute inside a trading system you already have. This guide shows where it fits, the technical traps it helps you avoid, and the exact steps to run through before you click buy or sell.

The cake: three layers, in this order

Nobody eats the icing on its own. It needs the other ingredients to complement it. Sentiment is the same: it's at its best on top of something solid.

The baseRisk management

How much you'll risk per trade, your maximum loss for the day and week, which markets and which hours. Decided before the charts are open. Get this wrong and nothing on top survives.

The spongeTechnical analysis

Strong weekly and daily demand and supply. The levels tell you where a trade can happen and where your stop belongs. This is the body of the trade.

The icingSentiment

Who is trapped, who is fuel, and where their stops and break-evens sit. It tells you when to pull the trigger at a level, or to leave it alone.

The kitchenFundamentals

Not a layer, the conditions you bake in: the macro backdrop (rates, yields, COT, risk mood) and the news calendar. They tell you which way the wind blows, and when to keep the oven shut.

The one rule

Sentiment is a trigger, never a signal on its own. It can confirm a trade at a strong level, or veto one. It can never start one.

Before you place a trade: the steps

This is the answer to "show me an example of the steps". Same order every time. If a step fails, you stop there, and the later steps never get asked.

Your plan is written down BaseRisk per trade (say 0.5 to 1%), maximum trades a day, the pairs you trade and the sessions you trade them in. If you're tired, busy or behind on the day, the plan says no trade.
Mark the strong levels SpongeWeekly and daily demand and supply only, the zones where price turned hard before. Each pair's 🎯 page in the QDB Sheet lists the monthly, weekly and daily pivots and opens as a price ladder, with distances in pips. You can see the same ladders live for 28 FX pairs and 7 indices on the free pair dashboards, with the crowd's positioning right beside them.
Price is at a level, and your setup appears SpongeWhatever your system calls a setup: a rejection candle, a sweep and reclaim, a pattern completing. No level, no trade. Price in the middle of nowhere? Close the chart.
Check the crowd IcingStraight after the levels, on the same pair dashboard: is the crowd positioned against your trade, or still piling in? Where are its stops, and is a minority in profit with a break-even nearby? Confirmed: go. Crowd on your side: skip it, or halve the size.
Read the market mood FundamentalsThe Risk Calculation tab reads the VIX and the crowd's big-picture positioning. Fear means safe-haven demand: JPY, CHF and USD tend to be bought, and AUD or NZD longs swim against the tide. Euphoria is the opposite: risk currencies and indices are favoured and safe havens sold. Gold isn't automatic: with US yields climbing, it can fall hard even when markets are fearful. Trade with the mood, or size down against it.
Check the news and yields FundamentalsAny high-impact news for either currency in the next few hours? A clean setup into a central bank decision is a coin flip. Then the backdrop: the US Yields tab tracks the same Treasury yield curve the New York Fed uses. Rising yields can override the usual playbook.
Place the stop BaseBeyond the level and beyond any stop cluster sitting on it, never on a round number. The SL & TP Planner tab works it out from the level.
Size it and set the target BaseSize the position from the stop, so a loss costs exactly what your plan allows. Target the next magnet: a stop cluster or the minority's break-even.

Why sentiment has a place at all

RSI, MACD, moving averages and Bollinger Bands are all made from the same thing: price. Stack four of them and you've asked the same question four times. They feel like confirmation, but they can't disagree with each other in any useful way.

Positioning is different information. It tells you who is already in, where they're hurting, and where their stops are. That's why it earns a place in the system, and why it belongs at the end: it's the one check your chart can't make for you.

Pitfall 1: the fake breakout of support

The textbook says: support breaks, you sell the break. It's the first trade most technical traders learn, and the crowd's favourite way to lose. Very often the "breakout" is a stop loss cluster being swept: the liquidity under the obvious low, taken before the real move.

The chart alone said "sell". The crowd said "everyone is already selling, and their stops are now above". That's the job of the icing. More on how clusters are hunted in the Stop loss clusters guide.

Pitfall 2: stops on round numbers

Why do so many traders put their stop at 1.3000, or 150.00? Not because they're lazy. Because they're busy, or overwhelmed: placing a trade between meetings, on a phone, with the kids shouting. A round number is the quickest thing to type. Multiply that by thousands of traders and round numbers become magnets, crowded with stops that the market can see.

The fix is time, not willpower

Plan the stop before you're in the trade, when you're calm. The SL & TP Planner tab works it out from the level, and the position size follows from it. If there's no time to plan the stop, there's no time to take the trade.

The snapback to the minority's break-even

The QDB Sheet's Breakeven Board shows two prices for each pair: the crowd's average short price and average long price. Whichever side is in profit has a break-even sitting away from price. When that winning side is the minority, price has shown a strong tendency to snap back to their break-even. Even a small minority gets punished.

Notice what happened there. The crowd was 80% long, so the contrarian read said sell. The snapback said buy, back up to the shorts' break-even. Both were "sentiment". The strong daily demand zone decided which one to act on. That's sentiment working inside a system rather than as a signal.

What the BE Memory tab has recorded

The 🧠 BE Memory tab logs every time a minority side moves at least 10 pips into profit, then watches whether price returns to that side's break-even within the time limit. The window started at 20 days; it was tightened to 10 days for a fairer, stricter test, because a lot can happen in 20 days. Figures at the time of writing (23 November 2026):

In Dave's testing so far, 18 of the 19 recorded minority break-evens (95%) have been revisited within the 10-day window. Subscribers can watch the live tally on the tab.

Read this before you trade it

It's a tested observation, not a guarantee. The history is short and the sample is small, prices are checked at each recalculation, and past results don't predict future ones. Most importantly, price reaching a break-even is not the same as your trade winning: it can hit your stop first. That's why the snapback is a target and a filter, used at a strong level with a proper stop, never a reason to enter on its own. The automated test that trades towards the break-even is still a work in progress, with rules being refined as it runs (for example, no trade when the stop would be under 15 pips away).

One trade, start to finish

Putting it together, illustratively. GBPUSD is falling into strong daily demand.

PlanIt's London session, a pair on the list, no trades yet today. Risk 1%.
LevelPrice reaches strong daily demand and dips under the obvious low, where a stop cluster sat, then closes back inside the zone.
KitchenNo high-impact GBP or USD news for six hours. COT isn't fighting the idea.
CrowdRetail is 70% short and added shorts on the way down (fuel). The failure-rate gate is open, and the shorts' stops are now bunched 60 pips above. Confirmed.
ExecuteStop beyond the zone and beyond the swept cluster, not at the round number. Size from the stop. Take profit at that cluster of shorts' stops: it's the magnet. Leave the rest of the move to the next setup.

If any one of those had failed, there would be no trade. Most days, that's the right answer.
