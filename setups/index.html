How a market makes the list

Sentiment on its own is never a trade. It only counts when the technical picture agrees and price is at a level worth trading from.

  • Crowd: at least 60% of retail traders on one side for FX pairs (55% for indices). We read it contrarian, so a crowd that is mostly long is a bearish sign.
  • QDB Score: clearly leaning the same way as the contrarian read: about a third of the checks or fewer for a sell, two thirds or more for a buy. The middle is no man's land and is skipped.
  • Strong level: a monthly or weekly pivot level, the weekly open, or a Daily Bollinger Band, on the right side of price (resistance above for a sell, support below for a buy) and no further than about one and a half average days' range away. Daily pivots and yesterday's high and low are too minor and don't count.
  • Crowd at an extreme (beyond 85/15): still listed, but marked wait. At extremes the crowd can stay right for a while, so let price confirm first.
  • Ranking: the stronger the score, the more one-sided the crowd and the closer the level, the higher it ranks.
