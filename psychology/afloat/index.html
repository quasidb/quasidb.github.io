Afloat or underwater: what following the plan does to your head

Picture two ships crossing the same sea. Same weather, same route, and the same iceberg waiting in the dark. One captain knows exactly what's in the hold and what it weighs. The other has packed in as much cargo as the ship will take, because more cargo means more profit. When the iceberg hits, only one of them is still sailing in the morning.

Two captains, one iceberg

In trading the iceberg is a news event. A surprise rate decision, a payrolls number nobody expected, a headline at 3 am. You can't stop it from being there. You can only decide, before you sail, how much damage it's allowed to do.

Underwater

The captain with no plan

  • Doesn't know the weight of the cargo. Position size is whatever "feels right", and it feels bigger after a win.
  • No watertight compartments. No stop loss, or one he moves "to give it room".
  • Never checked the weather. He didn't know the central bank was speaking today.
  • Massively overleveraged. One hit doesn't dent the ship, it breaks it in half.
Afloat

The captain with a plan

  • Knows the cargo to the kilo. Each trade risks the same 0.5 to 1% of the account, whatever happened last time.
  • Watertight compartments. A stop beyond the level and the stop cluster, so one breach floods one room, not the ship.
  • Checked the weather. Big news on the calendar means smaller, or no trade at all.
  • A line he won't cross. A maximum loss for the day and the week. Hit it and he goes back to port.

Sail it yourself

Set how much of the account you risk per trade, decide whether you use a stop, and set sail. Every voyage gets the same bad luck: four losing trades in a row, then the iceberg, a news event that gaps price straight through your stop. It's a rough run, but every trader meets one sooner or later.

    What the plan does to your head

    The maths is the easy part. What I've noticed over the years is how differently the two captains feel, and how that feeling decides the next trade.

    When the news hits
    Panic. Every tick is a decision, and every decision is made in fear.
    Calm. The worst case was decided last night, so there's nothing to decide now.
    After a loss
    "I need to make it back." The next trade is bigger, earlier and forced.
    A loss is a cost of doing business, already paid for in the plan. Next setup, same size.
    Waiting for a setup
    Can't wait. Being out of the market feels like missing out.
    Waiting is part of the job. The ship is fine in port.
    At night
    Checking the phone at 2 am.
    Asleep. The stop and the alerts are on watch.
    After a run of losses
    Confidence gone, or worse, doubling down to prove a point.
    Down a few percent, still in the game, still trusting the process.

    The real gift of a plan

    It isn't that you win more. It's that losing stops being an emergency. Once a loss can't sink you, you stop trading scared, and trading scared is what sinks most of us.

    Build your ship before you sail

    Everything that keeps you afloat is decided in port, never at sea.

    Weigh the cargoFix your risk per trade at 0.5 to 1% and size every position from the stop. The Risk Calculation and SL & TP Planner tabs in the QDB Sheet do the arithmetic for you.
    Fit the watertight compartmentsA stop on every trade, beyond the strong level and the crowd's stop cluster. Not on a round number, and never moved further away.
    Check the weatherLook at the calendar before every trade. Into a big release: reduce, wait, or stay flat. The calendar bot in the Discord posts the day's high-impact news each morning.
    Don't overload one holdThree trades that are all "short USD" are one big trade. The FX Pair Risk Matrix shows where your positions overlap.
    Know when to head back to portA maximum loss for the day and the week, written down. Hit it and you stop. The ship survives to sail tomorrow.

    A note from me

    I've been the first captain. Early on, I didn't know the weight of my cargo. I knew how much I wanted to make. A single news event taught me the difference, and it's a lesson I'd rather you learnt from a web page than from your account.

    The iceberg will come. It always does. Strict risk management doesn't stop it from coming. It just decides whether you're still afloat when it's gone.

    The one rule

    Never trade sentiment on its own. Use it only together with technical and fundamental analysis, and with strict risk management.
