No conviction after a bloodbath: why I miss the best long entries

Here's a confession. After a proper bloodbath in the indices, I can never find the conviction to go long. I know, on paper, that the best entries come when everything looks broken. But when the S&P has just dropped hard, my head is full of red candles, and I wait. Then I wait some more. By the time I feel ready, the optimal entry is long gone.

Why we freeze at the bottom

It isn't a lack of knowledge. It's how our brains are wired, and every trader I know fights the same three things.

Recency biasThe last thing we saw was a crash, so our brain expects another one. The most recent candles feel like the whole story.
The wound is still freshIf the sell-off hurt you, buying feels like walking back into the room where you got hit. Loss aversion makes the pain of being wrong again feel twice as big as the reward of being right.
Waiting for "confirmation"We tell ourselves we'll buy once it's clearly turned. But "clearly turned" means the move has already happened, and by then the crowd is buying too.

The cruel part is that the feeling and the opportunity run in opposite directions. Conviction tends to arrive at the top, not the bottom.

How the score is built

Six checks, each worth one point. Three come from the crowd's big-picture positioning and three from the markets themselves.

NowStatic view

Is risk sentiment, and is the live technical score, on the risk-on side right now? One point each.

YesterdayYesterday's static view

Was each of them risk-on yesterday too? One point each. A single good day isn't enough.

DirectionDynamic view

Are they improving over time, or getting worse? One point each, so the score sees the turn before my gut does.

The sentiment side reads the crowd's net positioning, its weekly average against the live figure. The technical side is a weighted score across the markets that tell you about risk appetite: the US and European stock indices, the VIX, US yields and the yield curve, high-yield bonds, oil, copper, the Nikkei and Hang Seng, and EURAUD. Above 50% leans risk-on.

Why it helps

It answers the question I can't answer honestly after a bloodbath: "Is the mood actually turning, or am I just scared?" The score doesn't know I lost money last week. That's exactly why I trust it more than myself in that moment.

How I use it, so I stop missing the turn

Let the score start the conversationWhen it climbs off 0 or 1 after a sell-off, that's my cue to start looking for longs on the indices, not to wait for my nerves to settle.
Still only at a strong levelThe score tells me the mood. The chart tells me where. I only buy at strong weekly or daily demand, with the stop beyond it and beyond the crowd's stop cluster.
Scale in, smallNo conviction? Then don't need it. Start with half size at the level and add the rest if the score keeps rising. A small position is much easier to take when you're scared.
Be wary when it feels easyAt 5 (Euphoria) and 6 (Dominance), when buying feels obvious, I tighten up. That's when leverage grows and caution looks foolish, so it's when the plan matters most.

A note from me

I still feel it every time. The sell-off happens, I tell myself "not yet", and a week later I'm watching the index rip without me. Knowing the psychology hasn't cured it. What helps is handing the first decision to something that can't feel fear. I still decide the level, the size and the stop. The score just stops me from hiding behind "I'm waiting for confirmation".

The one rule

Never trade sentiment on its own. Use it only together with technical and fundamental analysis, and with strict risk management. The score is a read on the mood, not a signal to buy.
