Trading psychology · about 7 minutes

It's easier to switch strategies than to change yourself

If there's one thing I've learned over the years, it's this. People switch strategies all the time, but they never hone their risk management. New system, a few wins, one big loss, blame the system, find a new one. Round and round. It's easier to change the strategy than to change ourselves.

Same trades, different trader

Every strategy the hopper dropped was the same strategy the other trader kept. It wasn't the system that failed. It was the sizing, the moved stops and the cut winners, and those came along to every new system.

Why we keep doing it

We want easy moneyA new strategy feels like a shortcut. Working on discipline, sizing and patience feels like hard work with no instant reward. So we shop for the shortcut.
Blaming the system is painless"The strategy stopped working" protects us. "I risked too much and moved my stop" doesn't. The error is putting the blame on something else, rather than ourselves.
The ego won't allow itNobody likes to admit they've been fooled, least of all by themselves.

"It's easier to fool people than to convince them that they have been fooled."

Often attributed to Mark Twain, though there's no record he actually said it. True either way.

The hardest person to convince is the one in the mirror. If you've switched strategy three times this year, ask yourself honestly: did the strategy fail, or did I?

Why it feels like the market is hunting your stop

It really can feel personal. Price drops to the exact pip of your stop, takes it, and turns. But the market doesn't know you exist. What most people don't realise is that they're not trading alone. They're part of a herd, a large group of traders who all read the same chart, see the same obvious low, and put their stops in the same place.

What to work on instead

Before you reach for the next strategy, give these a proper go. None of them are exciting. All of them work with any strategy.

Fix your risk per trade0.5 to 1% of the account, every trade, whatever happened on the last one. Most "broken" strategies are just oversized ones.
Put the stop where the herd isn'tBeyond the strong level and beyond the crowd's stop cluster. Never on the obvious low, never on a round number.
Let winners reach the targetIf your plan says 2R, cutting at 1R turns a winning system into a losing one, as the carousel shows.
Journal the cause of every lossWrite one line: "the setup failed" or "I broke a rule". After 20 losses, count them. It's usually the second one.
Give a strategy a fair trialJudge it over 50 to 100 trades at the same risk, not after one bad week. Five losses in a row is normal for a perfectly good system.

A note from me

I've watched this cycle for years, in myself early on and in plenty of traders since. The strategies change, but the losses keep coming for the same reasons. Strict risk management is the one skill that goes with you to every chart, every market and every system.

We know the vast majority of retail traders lose money, and we track how the crowd is doing right here on the site. So instead of becoming another statistic, practise and hone your risk management skills first and foremost.

Next time you're about to switch, stop. Take a moment to reflect on what happened, look within, and think about the longer term. Remember the tortoise and the hare: the hare didn't lose because he was slow. He burned himself out taking excessive risks and wasting energy, while the tortoise kept going at a pace he could sustain.

It happens to us all. Trading is that fine balance of knowing your risk and accepting the losses that come with it.

The one rule

Never trade sentiment on its own. Use it only together with technical and fundamental analysis, and with strict risk management.
